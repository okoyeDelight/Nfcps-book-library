# Tests

## Test 1 - Watch opens into a YouTube-style home feed and video page [sanity]
Viewport: mobile (375x667)
Covers: current installed shell delivery, /api/watch/feed trusted content, Home tab, topic chips, responsive video feed, watch page, related videos, save/share/like actions
Description: Verifies NFCPS Watch now opens directly into content and a tapped video behaves like a proper watch page rather than a promotional landing screen.

Steps:
1. Open NFCPS Watch from the installed/native-style app and confirm Home, Shorts, Subscriptions and You are available in the bottom navigation
2. Tap a visible video card and review the player, title, creator row, Subscribe control, Like, Share, Save and Up next area
3. Save and like the video, return to Home and continue scrolling beyond the first feed batch

Expected: Watch opens directly into a YouTube-style content feed, the selected video has a dedicated watch layout with related videos and actions, saved/liked state updates visibly, and more cards continue loading as the user scrolls.

## Test 2 - Shorts behave like a continuous vertical YouTube Shorts feed
Viewport: mobile (375x667)
Covers: Shorts tab, vertical snap scrolling, active YouTube playback, creator rotation, local Like/Save/Subscribe actions and automatic feed extension
Description: Verifies Shorts are a first-class navigation destination with one full-height video per swipe and no fixed terminal batch.

Steps:
1. Tap Shorts in the bottom navigation
2. Swipe through enough Shorts to pass the initial rendered batch
3. Like one Short, save another and subscribe to one creator while continuing to swipe

Expected: One Short occupies the viewing area at a time, swiping snaps vertically, new Shorts continue appearing beyond the initial batch, creators remain mixed when alternatives exist, and Like/Save/Subscribe state updates visibly.

## Test 3 - Subscriptions and You behave like a personal YouTube library
Viewport: desktop (1280x800)
Covers: local creator subscriptions, Subscriptions feed, You tab, Watch history, Watch Later and NFCPS reader bridge
Description: Verifies personal Watch state is organized into familiar YouTube-style destinations and persists on the device.

Steps:
1. Subscribe to two trusted creators and open the Subscriptions tab
2. Watch one video, save another to Watch Later, then open the You tab
3. Refresh the page and return to Subscriptions and You

Expected: Subscribed creators populate the Subscriptions feed, watched videos appear in History, saved videos appear in Watch Later, and the same local state remains after refresh.

## Test 4 - Watch search and degraded fallback never leave the experience unusable
Viewport: mobile (375x667)
Covers: search results, no-results guardrail, /api/watch/feed failure fallback and trusted seed content
Description: Verifies search has a clear empty state and a temporary Watch backend failure falls back to trusted curated content rather than a blank app.

QA Faults:
```json
[
  {
    "method": "GET",
    "path": "/api/watch/feed",
    "status": 500,
    "body_json": { "error": "simulated_watch_outage" }
  }
]
```

Steps:
1. Open NFCPS Watch while the Watch feed request fails
2. Confirm trusted fallback videos still render, then search for a nonsense phrase with no match
3. Clear the search and open one visible fallback video

Expected: The Watch home remains usable with curated fallback videos, a clear no-results state appears for the nonsense search, and clearing search restores tappable video content.

## Test 5 - Free e-books and reader remain available after the Watch redesign
Viewport: mobile (375x667)
Covers: Watch-to-reader bridge, /api/ebooks/catalog, visible free e-book shelf, /api/reader/book, in-app reader pagination and saved reading state
Description: Verifies the YouTube-style Watch redesign does not regress the connected NFCPS reading experience.

Steps:
1. Open You in NFCPS Watch and tap Read in NFCPS
2. Open a visible free Project Gutenberg title with Read now and advance to another page
3. Close the reader and reopen the same title from Continue Reading

Expected: The user can move from Watch to the e-book shelf, open a book inside NFCPS, navigate pages and return to approximately the saved reading position.
